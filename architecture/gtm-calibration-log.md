## Calibration Observation 001

### Diagnostic uncertainty can appear in different forms

**Date captured**  
September 2026

**Source context**  
An executive diagnostic conversation surfaced two different kinds of known friction.

In one group, leadership could identify the friction and already had an explanation for why it was happening, although the explanation had not necessarily been causally validated.

In the other group, leadership could identify the friction but did not know why it was happening.

The remediation goal existed in both cases. The diagnostic starting condition was different.

### Evidence classification

**Observation**

A single executive conversation revealed a practical distinction between recognized friction with a believed cause and recognized friction with no current causal explanation.

**Working hypothesis**

Diagnostic uncertainty may have at least two operational forms.

1. Leadership recognizes the friction and holds an explanation that still requires causal validation.
2. Leadership recognizes the friction but has no current explanation, so diagnosis begins with hypothesis generation and discrimination.

The first condition may be harder to diagnose when an unvalidated explanation has already hardened into organizational belief.

### Why it may matter

The existing v8 architecture can already process both conditions.

The Three Truths distinguish leadership belief from data and field evidence. The Causal Constraint Engine captures the leadership explanation, tests supporting evidence, generates alternatives, and searches for the unresolved dependency governing the outcome.

The open question is therefore not whether v8 can handle both states.

The question is whether explicitly distinguishing them would improve diagnostic entry behavior, questioning, or intervention quality across unrelated cases.

### What would strengthen the hypothesis

Evidence would become more meaningful if repeated cases show that the distinction consistently changes one or more of the following.

- which questions are asked first
- how leadership explanations are tested
- how quickly competing hypotheses are generated
- where false certainty enters the diagnostic
- which intervention is selected
- whether remediation improves because the starting state was identified correctly

### What would weaken the hypothesis

The distinction should remain local if repeated use shows that the existing evidence ladder, Three Truths, and causal inspection sequence already handle both conditions without any practical difference in diagnostic quality or sequence.

### Current decision

**Classification**  
Working hypothesis

**Canonical GTM v8 change**  
None

**Public architecture change**  
None

**Version decision**  
Hold and test

The observation is preserved for calibration. It should not change the operating thesis, six-lens architecture, Three Truths, Causal Constraint Engine, or public v8 architecture unless repeated evidence demonstrates that explicit treatment materially improves the framework.
