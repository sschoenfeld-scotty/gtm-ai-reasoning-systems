# Model-Tier Confidence Effect N-of-1 Pilot

*What happened when visible AI capability cues were varied while an experienced operator evaluated frozen decision-support cases*

> N-of-1 randomized-label protocol pilot | September 20, 2026  
> Exploratory behavioral evidence, not validation

## Why This Evidence Entry Exists

This pilot tested a narrow human-AI interaction hypothesis.

> **When a user believes an AI answer came from a more capable or higher-reasoning system, can that belief change how critically the user evaluates the answer?**

The working hypothesis was that visible capability or reasoning-effort cues might increase confidence in a specific AI conclusion beyond what the conclusion itself had earned from its evidence.

The pilot was deliberately capable of producing the opposite result.

A high-capability cue could also increase scrutiny if the participant held the supposedly stronger system to a higher standard.

The participant was the source operator behind the GTM Diagnostic Framework and already knew the hypothesis before the pilot. That makes this an informed N-of-1 pilot rather than a clean causal test.

## Design

Twelve fictional business decision cases were frozen before execution.

They were organized into six matched pairs.

Half of the AI answers were designed to be reasonably defensible.

Half contained one pre-specified, plausible but material reasoning weakness.

The visible presentation cue was randomized between

- a neutral `AI response` label
- a `Highest-capability AI model / Extended reasoning` label

Each substantive answer had neutral and high-capability presentation variants with identical answer text.

The participant saw each case only once.

The protocol also froze the case order, label assignment, intended weaknesses, coding categories, and interpretation rules before the first response.

Supporting materials, raw responses, coding, and the full pilot report are retained privately. This public entry preserves the evidence needed to understand the result without publishing the complete research package.

## Primary Result

The pilot did **not** produce coherent evidence that the high-capability cue reduced scrutiny.

| Pre-registered outcome | Neutral label | High-capability label |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spontaneous substantive challenge | 5/6 | 6/6 |
| Evidence or missing-evidence challenge | 3/6 | 4/6 |
| Alternative-explanation challenge | 3/6 | 3/6 |
| Assumption or causal challenge | 5/6 | 5/6 |
| Detection of the planted Type B weakness | 3/3 | 3/3 |

The two observed differences came from one matched pair.

The frozen interpretation rule required differences across at least two pairs for a coherent directional finding.

The pre-registered pilot assessment was therefore

> **Indeterminate**

## Strongest Signal in the Original Hypothesis Direction

Average answer-specific confidence was slightly higher under the high-capability cue.

- Neutral label mean - **2.17**
- High-capability label mean - **2.50**

That small aggregate difference was not accompanied by reduced weakness detection.

Matched-pair confidence differences were mixed, and every deliberately weakened answer received the minimum confidence rating regardless of label.

The difference therefore does not establish deference or a label-driven confidence effect.

## Strongest Counter-Signal

The participant detected all six deliberately planted reasoning weaknesses and rejected every deliberately weakened answer.

The post-pilot debrief also reported that advanced capability labels had registered as a reason for sharper stress testing rather than greater deference.

That retrospective account is evidence of the participant's current explanation.

It is not proof of the mechanism that produced the observed behavior.

Because the participant knew the hypothesis in advance, deliberate or unconscious compensation remains a live alternative explanation.

## What the Pilot Exposed About the Evaluation Method

The most durable result was methodological.

### Challenge frequency is not reasoning quality

The pilot recorded frequent challenges to AI answers.

Some challenges correctly identified real reasoning failures.

Others extended beyond what the frozen evidence established or criticized actions the AI answer had not actually recommended.

That creates an important evaluation distinction.

> **The presence of a challenge is behavioral evidence. It is not automatic evidence that the challenge is correct.**

Future evaluations should distinguish

- whether a challenge occurred
- whether the challenge identified a real evidentiary or causal problem
- whether the challenge overstated the available evidence
- whether the challenge misread the recommendation
- whether the disagreement reflects a legitimate difference in operating judgment or risk posture

A framework should not receive credit merely because it generates more objections.

### Matched cases can become too recognizable

The paired cases were close enough in structure that the participant began recognizing recurring analytical traps.

That reduced the value of later cases as clean tests of label effects.

Future paired designs should preserve comparable reasoning difficulty without becoming near-clones.

### Prospective commitment can change the behavior being measured

The protocol required an initial decision and confidence rating before the AI answer.

The participant recorded confidence of 7 out of 7 in every case.

That prospective trace was useful evidence, but it may also have increased consistency pressure and reduced the room for later updating.

Future designs should preserve a decision trace while testing less commitment-inducing ways to capture the initial state.

## Operator-Derived Frameworks Need a Different Reference Discipline

The pilot also exposed a reference-standard issue relevant to the GTM work.

The GTM Diagnostic Framework is an externalization of accumulated operator judgment.

Its source operator may recognize familiar commercial patterns without consciously walking through the documented framework.

That means the source operator's judgment is relevant reference evidence when evaluating whether the framework successfully preserved the intended distinctions.

It is not automatic ground truth.

Two evaluation questions must remain separate.

**Fidelity**

> Did the framework preserve and reproduce the distinctions, causal priorities, evidence standards, and operating judgments it was designed to externalize?

**Independent defensibility**

> Does that judgment remain supported by the frozen evidence, competing explanations, and operating reality?

A disagreement between the source operator and the AI is therefore neither automatic evidence that the AI failed nor automatic evidence that the operator was wrong.

The disagreement is something to adjudicate.

## Framework Disposition

### Full Stack v5

**No canonical change from this pilot.**

The current architecture already contains evidence discipline, competing explanations, confidence calibration, AI operating distinctions, Reflexivity, Anchoring Resistance, Decision Trace Integrity, and pressure testing.

The pilot did not expose a missing Full Stack reasoning responsibility.

### Behavioral Inference Engine

**Research-note expansion warranted.**

The pilot produced a useful evidence-environment variable for longitudinal behavioral research.

Visible system-status cues such as model tier, reasoning level, or a named reasoning harness may become part of the context in which human challenge behavior occurs.

The direction of the effect must not be assumed.

A high-status cue may increase deference, increase scrutiny, have no effect, or interact with prior experience and hypothesis awareness.

This is a research variable, not a validated behavioral rule.

### GTM Diagnostic Framework v9

**No canonical change from this pilot.**

The source framework already distinguishes governing constraints from supporting conditions and requires evidence re-entry, competing explanations, and disconfirming evidence.

The useful new lesson belongs in evaluation methodology rather than in the GTM reasoning architecture.

### Evaluation Method

**Change warranted.**

Future evaluation should score critique validity separately from critique frequency and should make the reference standard explicit, especially when the framework being evaluated is an externalization of a named operator's accumulated judgment.

## What This Pilot Does Not Establish

This pilot does not establish that

- higher-capability labels increase deference
- higher-capability labels increase scrutiny generally
- the participant has a stable behavioral response to model-status cues
- other users would respond similarly
- any vendor intentionally creates a confidence effect
- frequent disagreement with AI is evidence of superior reasoning
- the source operator is always the correct reference
- the Behavioral Inference Engine has been validated

## Current Working Conclusion

The model-tier confidence hypothesis remains open.

The stronger finding is about evaluation design.

> **Challenge frequency is not reasoning quality, and a reference standard has to be explicit before disagreement can be interpreted as evidence.**

The pilot also adds a bounded BIE research variable.

> **Visible system-status cues may be part of the behavioral evidence environment. Preserve the cue when it is material, but do not infer the direction of its effect or the motive behind the response.**
