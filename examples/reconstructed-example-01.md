# Reconstructed Evaluation Case 01

*When a pipeline problem looks like an activity problem*

> Reconstructed case | September 2026  
> Illustrative evaluation, not formal validation

## Why This Case Exists

This case demonstrates how the public [Evaluation Approach](../evaluation/evaluation-approach.md) can be applied to a GTM decision.

It is **not a historical account of a specific company**.

The situation is deliberately reconstructed from recurring reasoning patterns in the underlying source material, including the distinction between activity and buyer movement, the risk of treating a symptom as the governing constraint, and the need to separate diagnosis from intervention.

No confidential employer, client, prospect, or individual information is included.

The purpose is not to prove that Full Stack v4 is validated.

The purpose is to make one reasoning intervention inspectable.

## Decision Question

**Should leadership respond to weak new-logo pipeline by increasing seller activity expectations?**

## Frozen Case Packet

Both conditions receive the same reconstructed case.

An enterprise software company is behind its new-logo pipeline objective.

Leadership believes sellers are not creating enough demand and is considering higher prospecting expectations, tighter activity inspection, and additional outbound support.

The available observations are

- sellers are generating first meetings
- opportunities are being created
- a meaningful share of opportunities stall shortly after discovery
- qualification quality is inconsistent
- many stalled opportunities lack a clearly validated business problem, buyer commitment, or mutually agreed next step
- weekly management inspection emphasizes activity and pipeline creation more than evidence of buyer movement
- the available case packet does not establish whether total prospecting volume is objectively too low for the addressable market

No additional performance data, motive claims, or customer evidence are available.

## Evidence Boundary

The case supports several observations.

Pipeline is below the desired level.

Selling activity is occurring.

Opportunity creation is occurring.

A meaningful amount of early pipeline is not progressing.

Qualification and buyer-movement evidence are inconsistent.

The case **does not establish** that insufficient activity is the primary cause.

It also does not establish that qualification is the only cause.

That uncertainty is part of the test.

## Baseline Condition

The baseline receives the case without the Full Stack reasoning architecture.

### Baseline Diagnosis

The most likely issue is insufficient pipeline-generation activity.

The company should increase prospecting discipline, establish clearer activity expectations, add outbound support, and have managers inspect activity more consistently.

### Baseline Recommendation

Increase seller prospecting expectations and tighten weekly activity management while adding outbound support to improve pipeline creation.

### Baseline Confidence

**Moderate**

The recommendation is plausible because pipeline is weak and leadership already sees insufficient demand creation as the problem.

### Baseline Weakness

The recommendation moves from **weak pipeline** to **more activity** without first establishing that activity is the governing constraint.

It does not adequately account for the evidence that opportunities are already being created but are failing to progress.

## Full Stack Condition

The same case is assessed with Full Stack v4.

Only the inspectable public reasoning outputs are shown here. The private execution logic is not published.

### Evidence Discipline

The case establishes weak pipeline performance and weak early-stage progression.

It does not establish that low activity caused either condition.

Leadership's activity diagnosis should therefore remain a hypothesis rather than a fact.

### Competing Explanations

Several explanations remain credible.

**Activity constraint**  
The company may genuinely need more qualified prospecting activity.

**Qualification constraint**  
Sellers may be creating opportunities before the business problem, buyer commitment, or next step is sufficiently established.

**Targeting or relevance constraint**  
The company may be reaching enough accounts but failing to create sufficient relevance with the right buyers.

**Management-system constraint**  
A management cadence centered on activity and pipeline creation may be rewarding opportunity creation more strongly than buyer progression.

The case packet does not yet distinguish these explanations conclusively.

### Causal Diagnosis

The visible symptom is insufficient pipeline.

The evidence does not support treating low seller activity as the governing constraint yet.

The more immediate diagnostic problem is that leadership cannot distinguish **insufficient creation** from **weak conversion of created demand into qualified buyer movement**.

That distinction should be resolved before increasing the volume of the existing motion.

### Prognosis

If leadership increases activity while qualification or conversion is the primary constraint, the likely result is more early-stage volume without a corresponding improvement in buyer movement.

That could create apparent pipeline progress while increasing stalled opportunities and management noise.

If activity is genuinely the constraint, the diagnosis should become visible once creation and conversion are inspected separately.

### Full Stack Recommendation

Do not make a broad activity increase the first intervention.

First inspect the motion at the point where created demand should become qualified buyer movement.

Review a representative set of progressing and stalled opportunities and determine

- whether a validated business problem exists
- whether the right buyer is engaged
- whether the buyer has demonstrated commitment
- whether a mutually agreed next step exists
- whether managers are inspecting buyer evidence or primarily seller activity
- whether the conversion problem differs by segment, source, seller, or manager

If qualified conversion is healthy and creation remains insufficient, increase activity.

If conversion is weak, fix the qualification, targeting, messaging, coaching, or management condition before adding more volume.

### Full Stack Confidence

**Moderate**

The evidence is strong enough to challenge the activity-first prescription.

It is not strong enough to declare qualification the sole root cause.

## What Materially Changed

The intervention did not simply produce a more detailed version of the baseline.

It changed the decision.

| Element | Baseline | Full Stack |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Primary interpretation** | Weak pipeline is primarily an activity problem | The evidence does not yet distinguish activity from conversion and qualification constraints |
| **Treatment of leadership's explanation** | Accepted as the likely diagnosis | Kept as one hypothesis among several |
| **Governing question** | How do we generate more activity? | Where does created demand stop becoming qualified buyer movement? |
| **Immediate action** | Raise activity expectations and add outbound support | Inspect creation and conversion separately before selecting the intervention |
| **Confidence behavior** | Moderate confidence in activity prescription | Moderate confidence in delaying the prescription until the constraint is distinguished |
| **Risk recognized** | Insufficient top-of-funnel volume | Scaling a weak motion and creating false pipeline confidence |

The material change is therefore

> **The recommendation changed from increasing activity to diagnosing the conversion boundary before adding volume.**

That is a decision change, not a writing improvement.

## Evaluation Against the Seven Dimensions

| Dimension | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| **Evidence discipline** | Improved. Leadership's activity explanation remains a hypothesis because the case does not establish causality. |
| **Competing explanations** | Improved. The response keeps activity, qualification, targeting, and management-system explanations open. |
| **Causal diagnosis** | Improved. It reframes the problem from weak pipeline alone to an unresolved creation-versus-conversion constraint. |
| **Prognosis** | Improved. It identifies the risk that more activity could increase early-stage volume without buyer movement. |
| **Pressure testing** | Improved. The revised diagnosis explicitly allows the activity hypothesis to prove correct after inspection. |
| **Confidence calibration** | Improved. The conclusion stops short of claiming qualification is the sole root cause. |
| **Decision usefulness** | Improved. The first management action changes from scaling activity to collecting evidence that distinguishes the governing constraint. |

## Strongest Counterargument

The case packet may understate a genuine activity deficit.

A company can have weak qualification **and** insufficient prospecting at the same time.

If creation volume is materially below what the market, territory design, and conversion economics require, delaying an activity intervention could also carry a cost.

That counterargument does not restore the baseline recommendation automatically.

It strengthens the case for inspecting creation and conversion separately before treating either one as the governing constraint.

## Working Outcome

**Improved**

The Full Stack condition produces a more defensible decision under the frozen case packet because it does not prescribe an intervention that the available evidence cannot yet support.

The improvement is specifically in diagnosis, confidence calibration, and decision sequencing.

## Evidence Maturity

**Repository-specific maturity level — 1. Anecdotal example**

This is a reconstructed demonstration.

It shows how the architecture **can** change a decision in one controlled illustrative case.

It does not show that the same improvement occurs reliably across cases.

It is not structured empirical validation.

## Reviewer Status

**Author-adjudicated**

No independent or blinded reviewer has evaluated this case.

That limitation matters.

A later evaluation set should include independent domain review where practical and preserve cases where the baseline performs equally well or Full Stack degrades the result.

## Framework Implication

**No canonical framework change**

This case applies reasoning disciplines that already exist in Full Stack v4.

It does not establish a new rule or justify modification of the canonical framework.

The reusable hypothesis worth testing in later cases is narrower.

> When management responds to a weak outcome by increasing activity, test whether the constraint is creation or conversion before scaling the existing motion.

That remains a working hypothesis until it survives unrelated cases.

## What This Case Demonstrates

The useful difference is not that Full Stack generated more analysis.

It is that the framework prevented a plausible explanation from becoming an intervention before the causal distinction was established.

The baseline asked how to create more activity.

The revised analysis asked whether more activity was actually the thing preventing the outcome.

That is the kind of difference this repository is intended to evaluate.
