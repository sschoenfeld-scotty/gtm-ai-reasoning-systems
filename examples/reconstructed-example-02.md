# Reconstructed Evaluation Case 02

*When the baseline diagnosis is already good*

> Reconstructed case | September 2026  
> Illustrative evaluation, not formal validation

## Why This Case Exists

A useful evaluation set cannot be built only from cases where Full Stack v4 changes the answer.

That would reward the framework for being different rather than for being better.

This reconstructed case is designed to test the opposite condition.

The baseline diagnosis is already substantially correct.

The question is whether Full Stack produces a **materially better decision** or simply a more explicit version of the same one.

This case applies the public [Evaluation Approach](../evaluation/evaluation-approach.md).

It is **not a historical account of a specific company**.

No confidential employer, client, prospect, or individual information is included.

## Decision Question

**Should leadership try to improve poor proof-of-concept conversion by making POCs more technically impressive?**

## Frozen Case Packet

Both conditions receive the same reconstructed case.

An enterprise software company is seeing long proof-of-concept cycles and inconsistent conversion from POC to commercial commitment.

Leadership is considering adding more technical resources, expanding demo environments, and increasing the number of product capabilities shown during the POC.

The available observations are

- many POCs begin without a clearly documented business problem
- success criteria are often broad or still changing after the POC starts
- some buyers use the POC to understand what the product can do rather than to validate a previously defined requirement
- executive and commercial stakeholders are inconsistently involved before technical validation begins
- technical teams can often demonstrate that the product works
- successful technical demonstrations do not consistently result in a buying decision
- the case packet does not establish that technical product capability is the primary reason deals fail to convert

No additional win-loss data, buyer interviews, or competitive evidence are available.

## Evidence Boundary

The case supports several observations.

The technical validation stage is taking too long.

Success criteria are often weak or unstable.

Business and commercial alignment frequently precedes the POC inadequately.

Technical proof alone is not consistently producing buyer commitment.

The case does **not** establish that the product is technically weak.

It also does not establish that every failed POC originates in discovery.

## Baseline Condition

The baseline receives the case without Full Stack v4.

### Baseline Diagnosis

The primary problem is not that the POC needs to show more product.

The company is using the POC to perform work that should have happened earlier in discovery and qualification.

If the buyer has not agreed on the problem, required outcome, decision criteria, and commercial context before technical validation starts, a successful technical exercise may still fail to create a decision.

### Baseline Recommendation

Do not begin by adding more technical scope.

Tighten entry criteria for POCs.

Before a POC starts, require

- a validated business problem
- agreed success criteria
- clarity on the buyer and decision process
- a defined reason the POC is necessary
- an explicit next step if the success criteria are met

Use the POC to validate an established requirement rather than to discover what the buyer cares about.

### Baseline Confidence

**Moderate to high**

The case packet directly supports weak pre-POC definition and inconsistent business alignment.

### Baseline Strength

The baseline distinguishes **technical validation** from **buyer education and discovery** and changes the intervention accordingly.

It does not accept leadership's proposed solution of adding more technical content.

## Full Stack Condition

The same case is assessed with Full Stack v4.

Only inspectable public outputs are shown.

The private execution logic is not published.

### Evidence Discipline

The case establishes weak POC definition, unstable success criteria, and inconsistent commercial alignment.

It does not establish that technical capability is the governing constraint.

### Competing Explanations

Several explanations remain credible.

**Discovery and qualification constraint**  
The POC may be starting before the buyer's problem, priority, and decision requirements are adequately established.

**Decision-process constraint**  
The technical team may be validating successfully while the economic buyer, champion, or broader decision process remains unresolved.

**Technical constraint**  
Some POCs may genuinely fail because the product does not meet a technical requirement.

**Competitive or commercial constraint**  
A technically successful POC may still fail because of price, risk, procurement, competitive preference, or internal buyer politics.

The case packet does not fully distinguish these explanations.

### Causal Diagnosis

The strongest supported diagnosis is that the POC is frequently being asked to create clarity that should exist before validation begins.

That makes the technical stage responsible for education, discovery, qualification, and validation at the same time.

The POC may therefore be functioning as a substitute for an incomplete buying process rather than as a controlled test of an agreed requirement.

### Prognosis

Adding more technical resources or more product scope is unlikely to fix that problem if the buyer still lacks a defined business case and decision path.

It may make the POC more impressive while also making it longer.

If technical capability is genuinely the constraint in a subset of cases, stronger pre-POC criteria should make that failure easier to isolate rather than conceal it inside a broader evaluation.

### Full Stack Recommendation

Do not expand technical scope as the default intervention.

Strengthen the conditions required before a POC begins.

At minimum, establish

- the business problem being validated
- the required outcome
- the agreed technical and business success criteria
- the buyer roles involved in the decision
- why technical validation is necessary
- what decision follows if the criteria are met
- what evidence would constitute failure

Then use the POC to validate what discovery and education have already established.

Where POCs still fail after those conditions are met, investigate technical, commercial, competitive, and decision-process explanations separately.

### Full Stack Confidence

**Moderate to high**

The diagnosis is well supported by the frozen case packet.

The remaining uncertainty concerns which secondary explanation accounts for POCs that still fail after stronger entry conditions are applied.

## What Materially Changed

The Full Stack condition is more explicit.

It introduces competing explanations, a clearer prognosis, and stronger separation between supported diagnosis and unresolved alternatives.

But the central diagnosis and management action remain essentially the same.

| Element | Baseline | Full Stack |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Primary interpretation** | The POC is being used to compensate for weak discovery and qualification | The POC is frequently being asked to create clarity that should exist before validation |
| **Leadership's proposed intervention** | Do not add technical scope first | Do not add technical scope first |
| **Immediate action** | Tighten POC entry criteria and define business problem, success criteria, buyer, and next step | Tighten POC entry criteria and define business, technical, buyer, and decision conditions |
| **Role of the POC** | Validate an established requirement | Validate what discovery and education have already established |
| **Confidence** | Moderate to high | Moderate to high |
| **Decision** | Fix pre-POC discipline before expanding technical investment | Fix pre-POC discipline before expanding technical investment |

The wording and analytical structure improved.

The decision did not materially change.

## Evaluation Against the Seven Dimensions

| Dimension | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| **Evidence discipline** | Slight improvement. Full Stack states the unsupported technical-capability hypothesis more explicitly, but the baseline already avoids treating it as fact. |
| **Competing explanations** | Improved. Full Stack surfaces technical, decision-process, commercial, and competitive alternatives. |
| **Causal diagnosis** | No material change. Both conditions identify weak pre-POC discovery and qualification as the strongest supported issue. |
| **Prognosis** | Improved. Full Stack makes the risk of adding technical scope more explicit. |
| **Pressure testing** | Improved. Full Stack preserves the possibility that some POCs genuinely fail for technical or commercial reasons. |
| **Confidence calibration** | No material change. Both conditions use appropriately bounded confidence. |
| **Decision usefulness** | No material change. Both recommend strengthening POC entry criteria before increasing technical investment. |

## Strongest Counterargument

The case packet may overrepresent discovery and qualification failures.

If detailed win-loss evidence later shows that technically defined POCs routinely fail on product capability, integration, performance, or security requirements, then additional technical investment could be justified.

The current case does not contain that evidence.

Both conditions appropriately leave room for it.

## Working Outcome

**No material change**

Full Stack produces a more complete and pressure-tested explanation, but it does not materially improve the decision relative to the baseline.

Under the public evaluation methodology, that matters.

A framework should not receive credit merely because it produces more analysis.

The baseline was already good enough to identify the likely misdiagnosis and select the same first intervention.

## Evidence Maturity

**Repository-specific maturity level — 1. Anecdotal example**

This is a reconstructed demonstration.

It shows one condition in which the framework does **not** materially outperform the baseline.

That is useful evidence about evaluation behavior, not evidence of general system reliability.

## Framework Implication

**No canonical framework change**

This case reinforces an existing Full Stack principle rather than creating a new one.

The source framework already distinguishes

- education from validation
- visible symptoms from governing constraints
- clean execution from strong diagnosis

No new rule is required.

## What This Case Demonstrates

Full Stack should not be judged by how often it changes the answer.

Sometimes the first diagnosis is already strong.

In those cases, the architecture may add useful pressure testing without producing a materially better decision.

That is not failure.

It is evidence that **difference is not the evaluation standard**.

The standard is whether the reasoning intervention improves what should be believed, investigated, or done.

In this case, it did not change that threshold enough to count as a material improvement.
