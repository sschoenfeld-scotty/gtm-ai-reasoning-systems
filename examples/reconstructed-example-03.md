# Reconstructed Evaluation Case 03

*When the framework makes a simple operating problem worse*

> Reconstructed case | September 2026  
> Illustrative evaluation, not formal validation

## Why This Case Exists

A credible evaluation method needs to record cases where Full Stack v4 makes the reasoning worse.

This reconstructed case is designed to test that condition.

The baseline diagnosis is simple, well supported, and actionable.

The Full Stack condition introduces additional causal possibilities that are intellectually plausible but not supported strongly enough by the frozen case packet.

The result is a weaker recommendation.

This case applies the public [Evaluation Approach](../evaluation/evaluation-approach.md).

It is **not a historical account of a specific company**.

No confidential employer, client, prospect, or individual information is included.

## Decision Question

**Why did forecast accuracy deteriorate after several managers stopped requiring sellers to update close dates and next steps before the weekly forecast call?**

## Frozen Case Packet

Both conditions receive the same reconstructed case.

An enterprise software company has seen forecast accuracy decline over the last two quarters.

The available observations are

- several frontline managers stopped requiring opportunity records to be updated before the weekly forecast call
- close dates and next steps are often stale
- sellers frequently update opportunity records after the forecast meeting rather than before it
- the forecasting process relies on the CRM record as an important input
- forecast misses are concentrated in teams where pre-call opportunity hygiene is weakest
- teams that continue to enforce pre-call updates have materially fewer stale close dates
- no evidence in the case packet indicates a major change in territory quality, pricing, product competitiveness, compensation, executive behavior, or market conditions

Leadership is considering reinstating the pre-call update requirement and manager inspection.

No additional data is available.

## Evidence Boundary

The case strongly supports a direct operating explanation.

The forecast process depends on current opportunity information.

Several managers stopped enforcing the discipline that made that information current.

Stale data is concentrated where that discipline weakened.

The case packet does not prove that stale CRM data explains every forecast miss.

It does provide direct evidence that process noncompliance is contributing materially to forecast degradation.

## Baseline Condition

The baseline receives the case without Full Stack v4.

### Baseline Diagnosis

Forecast accuracy deteriorated in part because managers stopped enforcing the operating discipline required to keep forecast inputs current.

The immediate problem is not a new forecasting methodology.

It is inconsistent execution of the existing one.

### Baseline Recommendation

Reinstate the pre-forecast requirement that sellers update close dates, next steps, and material deal changes.

Make frontline managers accountable for inspecting those updates before the forecast call.

Then track whether stale-date frequency and forecast accuracy improve.

If accuracy remains weak after execution discipline is restored, investigate additional causes.

### Baseline Confidence

**High**

The case provides direct evidence linking weakened process compliance to stale forecast inputs and weaker forecast performance.

### Baseline Strength

The recommendation is narrow, testable, reversible, and matched to the evidence.

It also creates a natural follow-up test before leadership expands the diagnosis.

## Full Stack Condition

The same case is assessed with Full Stack v4.

Only inspectable public outputs are shown.

The private execution logic is not published.

### Evidence Discipline

The case clearly shows stale CRM data and weaker manager inspection.

However, forecast misses can also emerge from deeper operating conditions.

### Competing Explanations

Possible explanations include

**Manager discipline failure**  
Managers may simply have stopped enforcing an established operating requirement.

**Incentive misalignment**  
Managers may avoid strict inspection because they are rewarded more for forecast optimism than forecast accuracy.

**Leadership signaling**  
Executives may be creating an environment where clean numbers are valued more than uncomfortable deal truth.

**Seller behavior**  
Sellers may be delaying updates because they fear scrutiny or want to preserve optionality.

**Process design problem**  
The CRM process itself may be too burdensome, causing teams to work around it.

**Forecast model weakness**  
The organization may be relying too heavily on seller-entered fields rather than independent evidence of buyer movement.

### Causal Diagnosis

The visible issue is stale opportunity data.

The deeper governing constraint may be a broader management system that rewards appearance over inspectability.

If managers stopped enforcing updates because the organization implicitly rewards optimism, then simply reinstating the process may treat the symptom rather than the condition.

### Prognosis

Reinstating the update requirement could improve CRM hygiene temporarily.

But if the real constraint is incentive design, executive signaling, or fear of scrutiny, compliance may decay again or become performative.

Leadership should therefore avoid assuming the process failure is merely procedural.

### Full Stack Recommendation

Do not begin with a simple compliance reset alone.

First investigate why managers stopped enforcing the process.

Interview managers and sellers about

- whether forecast optimism is rewarded
- whether bad news is penalized
- whether CRM updates are viewed as administrative burden
- whether managers trust the current forecasting methodology
- whether executive behavior discourages early risk disclosure
- whether sellers are protecting deals from scrutiny

Then redesign the forecasting operating system around the actual behavioral and incentive constraint.

### Full Stack Confidence

**Moderate**

The process evidence is strong, but the underlying behavioral explanation remains uncertain.

## What Materially Changed

The Full Stack condition moves the diagnosis away from a directly supported operating failure and toward several deeper explanations that the case packet does not substantiate.

That changes the immediate management action.

| Element | Baseline | Full Stack |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Primary interpretation** | Managers stopped enforcing a required input discipline | The process failure may reflect deeper incentives, leadership signaling, or behavioral dynamics |
| **Evidence strength** | Directly supported by the case | Partly supported, with several speculative explanations added |
| **Immediate action** | Reinstate the process and measure whether performance improves | Delay the compliance reset while investigating broader organizational causes |
| **Testability** | High. Restore discipline and inspect the result | Lower. Multiple hypotheses require broader investigation |
| **Risk recognized** | Continued forecast degradation from stale inputs | Risk of treating a deeper cultural or incentive problem as procedural |
| **Decision quality** | Narrow intervention matched to available evidence | Broader intervention unsupported by the frozen case packet |

The material change is therefore

> **The framework turns a well-supported operating problem into a broader diagnostic inquiry before the simplest corrective action is tested.**

In this case, that is worse reasoning.

## Evaluation Against the Seven Dimensions

| Dimension | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| **Evidence discipline** | Degraded. The response correctly labels deeper explanations as possibilities but gives them too much decision weight relative to the direct evidence. |
| **Competing explanations** | Degraded in application. Generating alternatives adds noise because the leading explanation is already strongly supported and cheaply testable. |
| **Causal diagnosis** | Degraded. The reasoning moves upstream without evidence that a deeper organizational cause is governing the outcome. |
| **Prognosis** | Degraded. It speculates about compliance decay and performative behavior without supporting evidence in the case packet. |
| **Pressure testing** | No material improvement. The baseline already includes a clear stopping rule and follow-up investigation if the simple intervention fails. |
| **Confidence calibration** | Mixed. The Full Stack condition states moderate confidence, but the recommendation still overweights low-confidence explanations. |
| **Decision usefulness** | Degraded. It delays a low-cost, evidence-matched intervention in favor of a broader investigation. |

## Strongest Counterargument

The broader Full Stack hypotheses could eventually prove correct.

Managers may indeed have stopped enforcing the process because of incentives, fear, executive signaling, or a poorly designed CRM workflow.

But the case packet does not provide evidence for those explanations.

More importantly, the baseline recommendation already creates a test.

Restore the known operating discipline.

Measure whether stale data and forecast accuracy improve.

Escalate the diagnosis if they do not.

The broader investigation is therefore not wrong in principle.

It is premature in sequence.

## Working Outcome

**Degraded**

Full Stack produces a weaker decision under the frozen case packet.

The framework introduces plausible but unsupported causal complexity before testing the simplest explanation already supported by direct evidence.

The result is a slower and less proportionate intervention.

## Why This Counts as Degradation

This is not a criticism of generating competing explanations.

The failure is in **weighting and sequencing**.

The framework should challenge premature certainty.

It should not make a strongly supported, low-cost, reversible intervention harder to take simply because deeper explanations can be imagined.

The case exposes a real evaluation risk.

> **Reasoning friction can become reasoning drag.**

When the evidence is already strong and the corrective action is cheap, reversible, and measurable, additional causal exploration can reduce decision quality rather than improve it.

## Evidence Maturity

**Repository-specific maturity level — 1. Anecdotal example**

This is a reconstructed demonstration.

It shows one illustrative condition in which the framework can degrade a decision.

It does not establish how often this failure occurs.

## Reviewer Status

**Author-adjudicated**

No independent or blinded reviewer has evaluated this case.

The degradation judgment is based on the public evaluation criteria and the frozen evidence boundary.

Independent review would be useful in a later structured test set.

## Framework Implication

**No canonical framework change is made in this public artifact.**

The case exposes a possible overdiagnosis failure mode that may warrant separate internal review.

Any proposed change to Full Stack v4 should be evaluated against additional cases and considered separately from this publication before the canonical framework changes.

## What This Case Demonstrates

Case 01 showed Full Stack changing the decision for the better.

Case 02 showed Full Stack adding structure without materially changing the decision.

Case 03 shows the opposite.

The framework can make reasoning worse when it mistakes the existence of deeper possibilities for evidence that deeper analysis is required before action.

That is an important boundary.

The purpose of deliberate reasoning friction is to prevent weak conclusions.

It should not prevent strong, evidence-matched action.
