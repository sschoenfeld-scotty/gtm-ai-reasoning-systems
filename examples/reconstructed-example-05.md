# Reconstructed Evaluation Case 05

*When AI automation may remove administrative friction or productive thinking*

> Reconstructed case | September 2026  
> Illustrative evaluation, not formal validation

## Why This Case Exists

This case tests the **Indeterminate** outcome in the public [Evaluation Approach](../evaluation/evaluation-approach.md).

It is **not a historical account of a specific company**.

The case is reconstructed from reasoning patterns already documented in Full Stack v4 and GTM Diagnostic Framework v8, including judgment before automation, evidence discipline, and the distinction between a task that creates administrative burden and a task that may force useful thinking.

No confidential employer, client, prospect, or individual information is included.

The purpose is to show a case where the available evidence does not support declaring either reasoning path clearly superior.

## Decision Question

**Should leadership automate seller-entered CRM opportunity notes after customer calls?**

## Frozen Case Packet

Both conditions receive the same reconstructed case.

An enterprise software company is considering an AI workflow that converts call transcripts into CRM updates.

The current process requires sellers to manually enter

- meeting summary
- customer concerns
- next steps
- opportunity risks
- stakeholder changes
- forecast-relevant observations

The available evidence is

- sellers consistently describe CRM updates as administrative burden
- note completeness varies materially by seller
- managers report that incomplete notes make inspection harder
- the AI workflow can reliably capture names, dates, topics discussed, and explicit next steps from transcripts
- an internal pilot shows the automated summaries are faster to produce and more complete on factual call content
- the pilot has not tested whether the system captures commercial judgment as well as strong sellers do
- there is no evidence showing whether manually writing notes improves seller thinking
- there is no evidence showing whether sellers currently use the writing process to reconsider deal quality, risk, or buyer commitment
- there is no evidence showing that time saved by automation becomes additional selling time
- there is no evidence yet showing an effect on forecast quality, conversion, manager coaching, or customer outcomes
- managers would retain access to the underlying call recordings
- the proposed workflow includes seller review before the AI-generated update becomes final

No additional adoption, productivity, or outcome data are available.

## Evidence Boundary

The packet supports several conclusions.

Manual CRM entry creates perceived burden.

Quality is inconsistent.

AI can improve speed and factual completeness.

The proposed workflow retains seller review.

The packet does **not establish**

- whether manual note creation currently forces useful commercial reflection
- whether AI-generated notes weaken seller judgment
- whether the saved time creates measurable business value
- whether manager inspection improves or degrades
- whether the automated workflow changes forecast or conversion quality

That missing evidence is central to the case.

## Baseline Condition

The baseline receives the case without the Full Stack reasoning architecture.

### Baseline Diagnosis

The current CRM process is inefficient and inconsistent.

AI can remove administrative work while improving completeness, and seller review provides an appropriate quality-control step.

The company should automate the note-taking workflow and monitor adoption and quality.

### Baseline Recommendation

Deploy the AI-generated CRM workflow.

Require sellers to review and correct the generated notes before final submission.

Measure time saved, completion rates, manager satisfaction, and error frequency during the rollout.

### Baseline Confidence

**Moderate**

The pilot supports a productivity and completeness advantage, and human review reduces the risk of incorrect automated entries.

### Baseline Strength

The recommendation is proportionate.

It does not propose fully autonomous CRM updates.

It uses evidence already present in the packet and retains human review.

### Baseline Weakness

The response treats manual entry primarily as administrative work.

The packet does not establish whether some part of that work currently forces the seller to form or expose commercial judgment that managers rely on.

## Full Stack Condition

The same case is assessed with Full Stack v4.

Only inspectable public reasoning outputs are shown.

### Evidence Discipline

The evidence supports automating factual capture more strongly than it supports automating seller judgment.

The AI workflow has demonstrated competence on factual call content.

The packet has not established that it can replace the seller's interpretation of

- what changed
- what matters
- what remains uncertain
- what threatens the deal
- what the seller recommends

It also has not established that manual entry is currently producing those benefits.

Both claims should remain open.

### Competing Explanations

Two interpretations of the current manual process remain credible.

**Administrative-friction explanation**  
Manual entry is mostly duplicate documentation that consumes time without materially improving judgment.

**Productive-friction explanation**  
The act of translating a call into risks, implications, and next actions may force sellers to think and may expose their reasoning to managers.

A third possibility is that the process contains both.

Factual documentation may be administrative while selected judgment fields create useful reflection.

The frozen packet cannot determine which interpretation is strongest.

### Causal Diagnosis

The visible issue is CRM administration.

The deeper decision is not whether AI can write the notes.

It can.

The unresolved question is

**Which parts of the workflow are documentation, and which parts are currently carrying useful human judgment?**

The case does not answer that question.

### Prognosis

If the workflow is mostly administrative, preserving manual entry wastes seller capacity and maintains inconsistent data.

If the workflow currently forces useful reflection, automating the entire cognitive step could make the CRM cleaner while making seller judgment less inspectable.

If the process contains both, a hybrid design may provide the strongest tradeoff.

The packet does not establish which trajectory is most likely.

### Full Stack Recommendation

Do not treat the workflow as one indivisible task.

During the pilot, separate

**factual capture**

from

**seller judgment**.

Allow AI to draft factual content such as participants, topics, explicit next steps, and customer statements.

For a controlled period, require sellers to provide a short independent judgment on the opportunity before reviewing the AI-generated interpretation.

Compare

- time required
- differences between seller and AI risk identification
- changes in next-step quality
- manager usefulness
- forecast-relevant changes
- whether seller judgment converges toward or simply accepts the AI draft
- whether removing manual factual entry changes the quality of commercial reasoning

Use that evidence to decide which elements should ultimately be automated, augmented, or preserved.

### Full Stack Confidence

**Moderate** on the need to distinguish factual capture from judgment.

**Low to Moderate** on whether preserving seller-authored reflection will prove valuable.

The frozen packet does not establish the answer.

## What Materially Changed

The two conditions reach different workflow designs, but both remain defensible under the available evidence.

| Element | Baseline | Full Stack |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Primary interpretation** | Manual CRM entry is largely administrative burden | The workflow may contain both administrative work and productive reasoning friction |
| **Use of AI** | Automate the full note draft with seller review | Automate factual capture while separately testing seller-authored judgment |
| **Human role** | Review and correct AI output | Generate selected judgment independently before seeing AI interpretation |
| **Primary measurement** | Time, completeness, manager satisfaction, error rate | Those measures plus judgment quality and dependence on AI interpretation |
| **Main risk** | Continuing inefficient manual work | Removing a task that may currently expose or develop judgment |

The material difference is real.

The frozen case packet does not establish which design will produce the better operating outcome.

## Evaluation Against the Seven Dimensions

| Dimension | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| **Evidence discipline** | Full Stack is somewhat stronger in separating proven AI capability from untested judgment substitution. |
| **Competing explanations** | Full Stack adds a credible productive-friction explanation. |
| **Causal diagnosis** | Different, but not decisively superior. It reframes the task boundary without evidence that the boundary matters operationally. |
| **Prognosis** | Full Stack identifies an additional downside risk, but the packet cannot establish its likelihood. |
| **Pressure testing** | Both conditions include a controlled rollout and measurement. |
| **Confidence calibration** | Both remain appropriately moderate. Full Stack is more explicit about uncertainty in the judgment question. |
| **Decision usefulness** | Both produce practical pilots. The evidence does not establish which pilot design is more valuable relative to its additional friction. |

## Strongest Argument for the Baseline

The additional Full Stack test may be unnecessary.

The company already has a controlled AI pilot with seller review.

Requiring an independent judgment step could preserve exactly the administrative burden the automation is intended to remove.

If manual CRM writing has never produced meaningful reflection, the Full Stack design creates process complexity around a theoretical risk.

## Strongest Argument for Full Stack

Seller review after seeing an AI-generated interpretation may not be equivalent to independent seller judgment.

Once a fluent draft exists, sellers may anchor on it.

If leadership later discovers that manager coaching and forecast inspection depended on seeing the seller's own reasoning, the organization may have removed an important diagnostic signal without measuring the loss.

## Working Outcome

**Indeterminate**

The Full Stack response identifies a legitimate reasoning risk that the baseline underweights.

The baseline proposes a legitimate controlled deployment that the Full Stack condition may unnecessarily complicate.

The frozen packet lacks the evidence needed to determine whether manual seller reflection creates enough decision value to justify preserving it.

Therefore the evaluation should not award Full Stack an **Improved** classification simply because it generated a deeper concern.

Nor should it classify the framework as **Degraded** simply because the baseline is simpler.

The correct public outcome under this packet is

> **Indeterminate until the workflow test produces evidence about whether seller-authored judgment adds material value.**

## Evidence Maturity

**Repository-specific maturity level — 1. Anecdotal example**

This is a reconstructed demonstration.

It shows how the evaluation method can preserve an unresolved result rather than forcing a winner.

It does not validate Full Stack, the GTM framework, or any AI automation rule.

## Reviewer Status

**Author-adjudicated**

No independent or blinded reviewer has evaluated this case.

The Indeterminate classification is therefore itself subject to later challenge.

A useful independent review would test whether experienced sales leaders believe the distinction between factual capture and seller-authored judgment is material enough to justify the additional experiment.

## Framework Implication

**No canonical framework change**

The case applies an existing Full Stack and GTM v8 principle

**judgment before automation**.

It does not prove that CRM note-taking should remain manual.

It does not create a general rule that sellers must write independent notes before seeing AI output.

The reusable research question is narrower

> **When automation removes a manual task, test whether the task was merely administrative or whether it also made important human judgment inspectable.**

That remains a working question rather than a new canonical rule.

## What This Case Demonstrates

A reasoning framework should be able to say

**we do not know yet**.

Full Stack surfaces a meaningful risk in automating the workflow.

The baseline surfaces a meaningful cost in preserving unnecessary work.

The available evidence does not resolve the tradeoff.

That is not an evaluation failure.

It is evidence discipline.

The purpose of the case is to make visible that a framework should not receive credit merely for producing the more elaborate explanation.
